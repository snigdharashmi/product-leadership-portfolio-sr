# Textopedia — Business Case

**Product:** Textopedia  
**Tagline:** Discover, discuss, and access textbooks from around the world.  
**Version:** 1.0 (Portfolio)  
**Date:** October 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Textopedia is a global textbook discovery and community platform. It combines the social discovery strengths of Goodreads with practical access options (buy, rent, open, and eventually borrow), structured peer reviews, course-based context, and direct discussion with authors and other students.

The core problem is that students face high textbook costs, edition uncertainty, and fragmented information. They currently piece together recommendations from professor syllabi, Amazon/Chegg, and peer group chats. No single platform offers discovery, trusted peer validation, author engagement, and clear access paths in one place.

Textopedia starts with a focused MVP (discovery, reviews, discussions, course tagging, and aggregated access links) and grows into a durable community and access layer for academic materials.

---

## 2. Problem

- Textbook prices remain high; students actively seek cheaper alternatives (international editions, used, open).
- Edition compatibility is a major source of anxiety (“Will the international edition work for my class?”).
- Discovery is fragmented: professor syllabus → Amazon/Chegg → peer group chat.
- Peer reviews on clarity, difficulty, and value are more trusted than generic ratings, but hard to find in context.
- Authors have almost no direct feedback channel from student users after publication.
- Open textbooks and affordable options exist but are poorly discoverable relative to commercial titles.

---

## 3. Solution

A single platform where students can:

- Discover textbooks by course, university, subject, ISBN, or title.
- Read structured peer reviews (clarity, difficulty, value, edition compatibility).
- See clear flags for international editions, open textbooks, and price options.
- Discuss with other students and authors.
- Access buy, rent, and open links (with future borrow partnerships).

Authors and faculty can claim their books, reply to questions, and gather actionable feedback.

---

## 4. Target Users

**Primary:** Budget-conscious undergraduate students (e.g., Priya Sharma persona) who prioritise cost, edition compatibility, and peer validation.

**Secondary:** Textbook authors and faculty (e.g., Dr. Elena Vasquez persona) who want direct student feedback, visibility, and engagement.

**Tertiary:** Lifelong learners and institutional buyers (later phase).

## 6. Product Vision

Become the default social and discovery layer for textbooks worldwide — where students confidently choose the right (and most affordable) materials, and authors stay connected to the people who actually use their books.

---

## 7. MVP Scope (Now)

**Must-have for launch:**
- Search by title, author, ISBN, subject, course
- Book pages with structured + free-form reviews
- Discussion threads
- User accounts, profiles, and shelves
- Course / university tagging
- Aggregated buy / rent / open links with click tracking
- Seed catalog (open textbooks + popular titles)
- Basic author profiles

**Success signals:**
- ≥ 1,000 textbooks in catalog
- ≥ 500 registered users
- ≥ 200 reviews + discussion posts
- ≥ 15% of book-page visitors click an access link
- ≥ 30% of users return within 14 days

---

## 8. Roadmap Summary

| Timeframe | Goals | Key Initiatives |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| **Now** (0–3 months) | Launch usable discovery + review platform; attract early users; validate access-link demand | Core search, reviews, discussions, course tagging, access links, seed catalog |
| **Next** (3–6 months) | Improve relevance & engagement; grow content; establish early monetisation | International edition flags, author replies, simple recommendations, SEO, affiliate partnerships, university beta |
| **Someday** (6–12+ months) | Become default textbook community; expand access; unlock premium & institutional revenue | Mobile apps, shared notes, advanced recommendations, borrow partnerships, institutional dashboards, premium tier |

---

## 9. Business Model Options

1. **Affiliate revenue** (primary early model) — commission on buy/rent clicks.
2. **Freemium** — free core experience; premium for advanced notes, unlimited lists, priority support.
3. **Institutional / B2B** — university dashboards, course analytics, bulk open-textbook promotion.
4. **Open textbook partnerships** — featured placement or co-promotion with OER providers.

Early focus: affiliate + community growth. Later: institutional and premium layers.

---

## 10. Key Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Low initial content / cold start | Seed with open textbooks and popular titles; target specific courses for beta |
| Author participation is low | Make claiming and replying extremely low-friction; show clear value (feedback + visibility) |
| Users only use it once per semester | Build course shelves, notifications, and ongoing discussion value |
| Competition from Amazon reviews | Differentiate on course context, structured academic reviews, and author engagement |
| Monetisation is slow | Start with affiliate tracking from day one; measure click-through early |
